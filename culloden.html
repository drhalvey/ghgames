How to play: the government army

  • Pick your regiments. Tap a regiment on the moor or its button under the picture, and orders go to just those men. ALL commands everyone. Keys 1 to 9 and 0 pick regiments, the backtick key picks all.
  • The cannonade. The Jacobites stand on the far side of the moor. Press FIRE THE GUNS each time your ten little guns are loaded. ROUNDSHOT reaches far. When the clans charge, switch to GRAPESHOT: a can of musket balls that is deadly under 300 yards and useless further out.
  • Firing by ranks. Each battalion is three ranks deep and fires one rank at a time, always in this order: MAKE READY, then REAR RANK, PRESENT, REAR RANK, FIRE!, REAR RANK, RECOVER, then the same for the MIDDLE RANK and the FRONT RANK, then start again. The big button gives the next word of command to the battalions you have picked; the row above it shows where they are in the drill.
  • Wait a moment after PRESENT so the muskets are level, or the volley comes out ragged, but don't hold them too long. A rank that has recovered reloads while the others fire, so keep the drill rolling. Fire rear, middle and front all cleanly with the clans close for PERFECT RANK FIRE. Start the rear rank at about 100 yards: the charge is fast.
  • When the clans reach your line, tap NEW BAYONET DRILL when the marker is in the green. Each man thrusts at the Highlander attacking the soldier on his right, under the sword arm where the targe does not cover him.
  • The second line. Sempill's and Bligh's stand behind. They cannot shoot through their own men, so if the clans burst through, ADVANCE them and fire. WOLFE'S, WHEEL! swings Wolfe's regiment forward on the left so it fires along the side of the charge.
  • DRAGOONS: the Campbell militia knock gaps in the Culwhiniac walls and Hawley's dragoons ride round behind the Jacobite right. It takes a while, so send them early.
  • Watch each battalion's discipline bar. Nervous men fire before the word is given, or run. STEADY, LADS! calms them. If three regiments are overrun, the battle is lost.
  • Camera. Drag to swing round, pinch or wheel to zoom, two fingers or right-drag to move. 🎥 switches Auto, Free and the soldier's view. ⏩ speeds up the battle and you can watch the replay after.

How to play: the Jacobite army

  • Pick your clans. Tap a clan on the moor or its button. ALL commands the whole line. Keys 1 to 8 pick clans, 0 picks all.
  • The cannonade. Cumberland's guns are far better than yours, and every minute you wait, roundshot ploughs through your ranks. KNEEL to lose fewer men, but Highlanders hate waiting.
  • Each clan has a fury bar. When it fills, that clan charges on its own, without orders, and gets shot to pieces alone. HOLD THEM! calms them down.
  • CLAYMORE! sends the Highland charge across the moor. Charge the whole line together for a WHOLE LINE CHARGE. The bog in front of the MacDonalds slows them down.
  • At about 40 yards, FIRE! one volley, then throw down your muskets and draw broadswords. The redcoats fire too, so get through their smoke before they reload.
  • At the red line, tap SWORD AND TARGE when the marker is in the green to break the regiment in front. Overrun three regiments and the battle is yours.
  • Watch for Hawley's dragoons coming round the walls behind your right. SECOND LINE sends Lord Lewis Gordon's and Ogilvy's men to face them.

What really happened

  • Culloden was the last pitched battle fought on British soil. Prince Charles Edward Stuart, "Bonnie Prince Charlie", wanted to win back the throne for his family, the Stuarts. His Jacobite army faced the government army of the Duke of Cumberland, son of King George II, on Drummossie Moor near Inverness.
  • It was not England against Scotland. There were Scots on both sides, including the Royal Scots, the Royal Scots Fusiliers and the Campbells of Argyll.
  • The Jacobites were tired and hungry after a failed night march. They stood on open, wet ground that suited Cumberland's guns. A cold wind blew sleet and smoke into their faces.
  • The Jacobite guns fired first, but they had few trained gunners. Cumberland's ten 3-pounders replied for perhaps 10 to 15 minutes while the clans waited for the order to charge.
  • The Mackintoshes of Clan Chattan charged first, then the right wing. The boggy ground and the road pushed the charge to its right, and it crashed into Barrell's and Munro's regiments.
  • Cumberland had drilled his men to thrust their bayonets at the Highlander attacking the man to their right. Barrell's were driven back, but Sempill's and Bligh's in the second line fired into the Highlanders who broke through, and Wolfe's fired along their flank.
  • On the left, the MacDonalds had further to go through bog. They halted short of the line. Alexander MacDonald of Keppoch charged forward with a few men and was killed.
  • Within an hour the Jacobites were running. The Irish Picquets and the Royal Écossais, soldiers in French service, covered the retreat while dragoons rode round through the walls. About 1,200 to 1,500 Jacobites were killed. The government lost 50 killed and 259 wounded.
  • The game has every battalion fire by ranks. On the day most regiments used platoon firing: small groups fired one after another along the line, and the front rank kept its fire for the moment the Highlanders arrived.
  • After the battle, Cumberland's army treated the Highlands very harshly, and many people later remembered him that way. Laws banned Highland weapons and, for a time, tartan. The Prince escaped to France months later, helped by Flora MacDonald.
