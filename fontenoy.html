How to play

  • Two lines, face to face. Your battalions stand three ranks deep in a long line. The enemy line does the same. Whoever keeps firing steadily and keeps their nerve wins.
  • Pick your battalions. Tap a battalion on the field or its button. Orders go to the ones you pick. ALL commands the whole line. Keys 1 to 9 and 0 pick battalions, the backtick key picks all.
  • Two ways to fire (choose on the start screen). By ranks: MAKE READY, then REAR RANK present, fire, recover, then the MIDDLE RANK, then the FRONT RANK, then start again. Battalion volleys: MAKE READY, PRESENT, FIRE! with every rank at once, then reload.
  • The big button gives the next word of command; the row above it shows where you are in the drill. Wait a moment after PRESENT so the muskets are level, or the fire comes out ragged, but don't hold them too long.
  • Range matters. Beyond 100 yards almost every ball misses. At 40 to 60 yards a good volley tears the enemy line apart. A clean drill with the enemy close is PERFECT FIRE.
  • ADVANCE marches the battalions you pick forward. They halt by themselves 35 yards from the enemy, or when you give MAKE READY, or when you press it again.
  • FIRE FIRST, GENTLEMEN! Once a battle, when the lines are close, you can politely invite the enemy to fire first. They fire too early and too high, your men brace, and your next volley lands while they are reloading.
  • Each battalion has a discipline bar. Every man hit lowers it. Nervous men fire before the word is given, and a battalion at zero runs away. STEADY! calms everyone. Tap RELOAD DRILL in the green to load faster.
  • When three battalions of a side break, its reserves march up: the Irish Brigade and Normandie for the French, the 20th, 25th and the Hanoverians for the British. Break six enemy battalions to win.
  • Camera. Drag to swing round, pinch or wheel to zoom, two fingers or right-drag to move. 🎥 switches Auto, Free and the soldier's view. ⏩ speeds up the battle, and you can watch the replay after.

What really happened

  • Fontenoy was fought on 11 May 1745, near Tournai in what is now Belgium, during the War of the Austrian Succession. A French army under Marshal Maurice de Saxe faced an allied army of British, Hanoverian, Dutch and Austrian troops under the Duke of Cumberland. King Louis XV of France watched.
  • The French held a line between the village of Fontenoy and the Bois de Barry wood, with cannon in the village and in an earthwork called the Redoute d'Eu.
  • After attacks on Fontenoy and the redoubt failed, Cumberland sent about 15,000 British and Hanoverian infantry straight up the slope between them in one huge column, with cannon firing into both sides of it.
  • At the top they met the French Guards and Swiss Guards. A story first told by the writer Voltaire says that Lord Charles Hay of the British Guards stepped forward, raised his hat and invited the French to fire first. Nobody is sure it really happened like that.
  • The French Guards fired first and too early. The British volleys that answered hit hundreds of men and broke the French front line.
  • The column pushed on into the French camp but was attacked from three sides: French infantry, the Irish Brigade, cavalry charges and cannon brought up to fire at close range. Short of ammunition, it marched back down the slope in good order.
  • The French won. Both sides lost very heavily: about 7,000 to 8,000 French and well over 7,000 allied soldiers were killed, wounded or captured.
  • The game lets each side fire either by ranks or by whole-battalion volleys. On the day the British fired by platoons, small groups one after another along the line.
