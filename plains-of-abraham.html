How to play

  • French sharpshooters hide in the bushes and cannon fire from far away. Lie down while you wait so fewer men get hit.
  • When the drums beat, the French columns attack. Stand up, then hold your fire. Shooting too far away hardly hits anyone.
  • Watch the range. At about 40 yards press MAKE READY, then FIRE! If every battalion fires at the same moment you get a PERFECT VOLLEY, which smashes the French courage.
  • Waiting is scary. Each battalion has a discipline bar. Low discipline means they fire raggedly, or fire without orders, or even run away. Shout STEADY, LADS! to calm them.
  • After a volley, tap RELOAD DRILL when the white marker is in the green to reload faster. Your smoke spoils the next shot, so ADVANCE through it.
  • When the French are wavering, CHARGE! Charge too early against steady troops and your men get pushed back.
  • Win to earn shillings and buy upgrades. Get all three stars with a perfect volley, no broken battalions and nobody firing without orders.

What really happened

  • On the night of 12 September 1759, General James Wolfe's British army climbed the steep cliffs at the Anse-au-Foulon and formed up on the Plains of Abraham, just outside the walls of Quebec.
  • Wolfe stretched his line across the plain only two ranks deep instead of the usual three. While they waited, his men lay down to shelter from Canadian militia and First Nations warriors firing from the bushes and cornfields.
  • Many soldiers loaded two musket balls for a deadlier first shot at close range.
  • The Marquis de Montcalm's French army attacked in columns. As they came on, their ranks became jumbled when militia stopped to fire and dropped down to reload.
  • The British held their fire until the French were about 40 yards away. Their volley went off so close together that it sounded like one huge gun. The historian Sir John Fortescue later called it "the most perfect volley ever fired on battlefield".
  • The British stepped forward through the smoke, fired again, then charged. The 78th Fraser's Highlanders charged with broadswords. The main fight lasted only about fifteen minutes.
  • Both generals were mortally wounded: Wolfe on the field, Montcalm the next morning. Quebec surrendered five days later.
